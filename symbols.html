National symbols

The flag and the anthem of Irad were adopted by the Provisional Government in May 1992 and confirmed by the State Symbols Act of 1993. They have been kept by every Iradi administration since, and are used on both sides of the Qara-Chik line. The State Emblem was added to the Act in 2009.

Description

A crimson field bearing a roundel in ivory: a white stallion in gait facing the hoist, a five-pointed star above it, an inverted horseshoe on the hoist side, the words erkin bol (“be free”) in Old Turkic (Orkhon) letters arched along the top, and two ears of wheat. Proportions 1:√2. Reference colours: crimson #AA0000, ivory #FFF6D5. The official description was issued by the Department of Public Information in 2015.

Meaning

  • Crimson: the banners of the 1916 revolt.
  • The stallion: Irad's horses, the Chongtor and the Akyel, and the land where the horse was first tamed.
  • The star: Polaris, called Irad, which gave the country its name.
  • The inverted horseshoe: the Fallen Rider, in memory of the martyrs of 1916, of the purges and of both civil wars.
  • The inscription: the call to freedom.
  • The wheat: the farming life of the river valleys.

The State Emblem

Adopted by the Mejlis in 2009 as an amendment to the State Symbols Act. In the centre is the tündük, the crown of the yurt, against the sky, its roof poles spreading like rays: one roof over all the people of Irad. Two horses rear towards it, a Chongtor of the mountains and an Akyel of the valleys. On the crimson ring are the name of the country in Latin and Cyrillic letters either side of the Pole Star, the words erkin bol in Orkhon letters either side of the Fallen Rider's horseshoe, and the koshkar müyüz pattern.

The emblem appears on passports, on the acts of the Mejlis and the courts, and on the seals and letterheads of the ministries.

The seal of the Office

The roundel of the flag, on a crimson disc, served as the seal of the ISEZ from 2007 until the State Emblem was adopted, and remains the emblem of the Office of the Governor: on its seal, its documents and Government House. Neither emblem may be used commercially without the permission of the Department of Public Information.

The anthem

“The Spirit of Irad” (Irad Ruhu). Music by Asan Jumabaev, words by Cholpon Myrzakulova. First sung at the proclamation of the Republic in Talas on 11 March 1992.

Three stanzas and a refrain: the mountains, rivers and horse herds of Irad; those who died for the country; and the star that guides the nation. The official text is in Iradi, with an official English version (1993). Schools sing the first stanza and the refrain.

The anthem is performed on Irad Day, at the opening of each session of the Mejlis, at the inauguration of the Governor and at military ceremonies.

National day

Irad Day, 11 March, marks the proclamation of the Republic of Irad in Talas in 1992. It is the national holiday, kept on both sides of the line, with the raising of the flag in Talas, races and the final of the President's Cup at the Irad State Hippodrome.
